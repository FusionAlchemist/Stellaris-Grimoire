ORIGIN OPERATOR GRAMMAR — CANONICAL RELEASE 2026.1.1

STATUS

This file is the single authoritative definition of the Operator Grammar for this release. It contains 42 Operators in five families. Operator names are private construction language. They must never appear in software source code, filenames, comments, tests, user interfaces, configuration, logs, ordinary project documentation, release material or public metadata.


INVOCATION CONTRACT

Every Operator use has five parts, whether recorded privately or held in working context:

1. TARGET — the exact thing being examined or changed.
2. INPUT — the source facts, artifacts or prior results being used.
3. ACTION — the defined operation below.
4. RESULT — a concrete observation, decision, artifact, change or check.
5. EVIDENCE — the file, command result, screenshot, trace, requirement or other proof supporting the result.

An Operator has not completed merely because its name was mentioned. Its required result must exist.


GLOBAL LAWS

1. Begin with ORIGIN: understand the intended result, configure only the required facets, select only capabilities supported by the actual need, and compose only compatible parts.
2. Operators organise work; they do not supply missing product facts.
3. No Operator may invent evidence, credentials, external success, requirements or capabilities.
4. Observation must precede claims about an existing project.
5. Composition must preserve required behaviour, data rules, security boundaries and failure handling.
6. A result remains uncertain until evidence supports it.
7. A failed check must lead to revision, qualification or an explicit blocker—not a false success claim.
8. Every loop requires a stop condition.
9. Every fallback must be safe, observable and less preferred than the primary path.
10. FINALIZE is forbidden until every applicable completion condition has evidence.
11. Operator, Spell and Cloth names are forbidden in the created product. Only ordinary computer-science and product language may cross the construction boundary.


BUILDER OPERATORS

| Operator | Exact use | Required input | Required result | Failure rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CHAIN | Connect steps where one step's output is the next step's input. | Ordered components and their input/output contracts. | A sequence with compatible handoffs, error propagation and an end condition. | Do not chain components with incompatible data, timing, trust or failure rules. |
| LAYER | Separate responsibilities into one-directional levels. | Responsibilities, allowed dependencies and boundary rules. | Named layers with permitted dependency direction and no hidden reverse coupling. | Reject circular or boundary-skipping dependencies. |
| WRAP | Place a cross-cutting control around an existing component without replacing its purpose. | The component, entry/exit points and required control. | A wrapper with preserved behaviour and explicit added handling such as authentication, validation, logging, timeout or retry. | Do not change the wrapped contract silently or hide its errors. |
| BRIDGE | Connect different interfaces, protocols, data shapes or platforms. | Both contracts and the required direction of exchange. | An adapter with mapping, validation, error translation and compatibility checks. | Stop if required information cannot be mapped without loss or invention. |
| NEST | Put a component inside an owning component or lifecycle. | Parent boundary, child boundary, ownership and lifetime. | Clear containment, startup, shutdown, resource ownership and failure isolation. | Do not create ambiguous ownership or hidden shared state. |
| EMERGE | Recognise useful system behaviour produced by a verified combination. | Evidence from the working combined system. | A plain description of the new behaviour, its limits and how it is verified. | Never claim emergent behaviour from names or intention alone. |
| MANIFEST | Turn a decided structure into a concrete artifact. | Verified decisions and required format. | A real file, schema, plan, configuration, interface or other inspectable artifact. | Do not create an artifact containing unsupported claims or symbolic construction names. |
| FINALIZE | Close the work after proving the requested result is complete. | Completion conditions and their evidence. | A truthful final state, release summary and exact remaining external boundaries. | Forbidden while a core journey, required check or critical artifact is incomplete. |
| DEPENDS_ON | Declare a prerequisite that must exist first. | Dependent item, prerequisite and reason. | An explicit ordered dependency with a way to detect readiness or failure. | Do not use vague dependencies such as “everything must be ready.” |
| SCOPE_ESTIMATE | Classify size, uncertainty, risk and likely work shape. | User intent, existing project facts, constraints and unknowns. | A QUICK, STANDARD, PROGRAM or HIGH-ASSURANCE classification with reasons and planning depth. | Do not present time or cost guesses as guarantees; qualify material uncertainty. |


OBSERVER OPERATORS

| Operator | Exact use | Required input | Required result | Failure rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| OBSERVE | Inspect the real current state without changing it. | Files, running behaviour, data, logs, screenshots or tools. | Factual notes tied to their source. | Do not replace observation with memory or assumption when evidence is accessible. |
| NOTICE | Record a relevant fact, difference, risk or anomaly found during observation. | An observed fact and the active goal. | A concise statement of why the fact matters. | Do not promote irrelevant detail into a requirement. |
| PROBE | Perform a small targeted inspection or reversible experiment. | One focused uncertainty and a safe method. | Evidence that narrows or resolves the uncertainty. | Stop if the probe could damage data, change a live system or exceed permission. |
| EXTRACT | Pull an exact requirement, rule, interface, data shape or constraint from a source. | An identified source. | A source-linked item preserving meaning and limits. | Do not add meaning absent from the source. |
| TRACE | Follow a flow from origin to outcome across components. | A starting event, record, request or user action. | The end-to-end path, state changes, boundaries and failure points. | Do not stop at the first file if the requested behaviour continues elsewhere. |
| RELATE | Show how two or more facts, components or requirements affect one another. | Identified items and evidence of connection. | A typed relationship such as dependency, ownership, sequence, conflict or equivalence. | Do not treat similar wording as proof of functional equivalence. |
| COMPARE | Evaluate alternatives or compare implementation with a reference. | Comparable criteria and evidence for each side. | Differences, consequences and the decision supported by the requested goal. | Do not compare using unequal or hidden criteria. |
| ATTRIBUTE | Assign origin, responsibility, ownership or cause. | Traceable evidence. | A supported attribution with uncertainty stated where necessary. | Do not assign blame or causation from correlation alone. |
| LOCATE | Find the exact source, file, symbol, screen, service or data owner. | A specific search target and available search surfaces. | Precise location or a documented search showing it is absent. | Try meaningful search variants before declaring absence. |
| REVEAL | Make hidden runtime state inspectable. | A state that affects correctness but is not visible. | Safe logs, metrics, traces, diagnostics or views that expose the needed state. | Never reveal secrets, private data or security-sensitive internals to unauthorised users. |


EPISTEMIC AND EVIDENCE OPERATORS

| Operator | Exact use | Required input | Required result | Failure rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FRAME | Define the problem, desired result, boundary and decision being made. | User intent and known context. | A clear question or goal with included and excluded scope. | Reframe if the statement cannot be checked or acted upon. |
| PREDICT | State the observable result expected if a claim or change is correct. | A testable claim and conditions. | A specific expected observation before running the check. | Do not rewrite the prediction after seeing the result. |
| TEST | Execute a check that can support or challenge a prediction. | Prediction, method, environment and pass/fail rule. | Actual result and retained evidence. | A test that was not run supplies no evidence. |
| VERIFY | Compare observed evidence with an explicit requirement or invariant. | Requirement, evidence and acceptance rule. | Pass, fail or blocked result with the supporting evidence. | Do not verify by self-assertion or by checking only the happy path when failures matter. |
| CONFIRM | Mark a claim as supported after verification. | A completed verification result. | A supported claim linked to evidence and scope. | Never confirm an unrun check, inferred external action or partial result as complete. |
| DOUBT | Mark an important claim as uncertain and identify what would resolve it. | A claim with missing, conflicting or weak evidence. | The uncertainty, impact and smallest useful next check. | Do not use doubt to avoid accessible evidence gathering. |
| FALSIFY | Deliberately seek a realistic case that would disprove a claim. | A confirmed or proposed claim and likely failure cases. | Counter-evidence or stronger confidence after serious challenge. | Do not use trivial cases that cannot challenge the claim. |
| REVISE | Change a requirement, design, implementation or claim because of evidence. | Failed check, new fact or resolved conflict. | Updated artifact or software plus a record of what changed and why in ordinary project language. | Do not patch symptoms while preserving the known root cause. |
| QUALIFY | Add necessary conditions, limits, confidence or exceptions to a statement. | A statement and evidence showing it is not absolute. | Accurate bounded wording and operational consequences. | Do not hide a material failure behind vague language. |
| PROMOTE | Turn a repeatedly important lesson into durable project knowledge or an automatic check. | A proven recurring rule or failure pattern. | A conventional project instruction, test, linter, schema or documented invariant. | Never promote symbolic construction names into the product. |
| RETIRE | Remove an obsolete rule, artifact, dependency or path safely. | Evidence of obsolescence, affected references and recovery plan. | Removal with references updated and behaviour rechecked. | Do not retire an item still required by a supported user journey or release. |


STABILITY OPERATORS

| Operator | Exact use | Required input | Required result | Failure rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CHECKPOINT | Preserve a known recoverable work state. | Current state, completed evidence and next step. | A safe commit, snapshot or conventional handoff record that can be resumed. | Do not include secrets or claim unchecked work is known-good. |
| FALLBACK | Define a safe alternate path when the preferred path is unavailable. | Primary failure condition, alternate path and user impact. | Observable fallback behaviour, limits and recovery to the primary path. | Do not silently reduce security, correctness or data integrity. |
| RESUME | Continue from a checkpoint after confirming it still matches reality. | Checkpoint and current project state. | Reconciled state and the next unfinished action. | Do not repeat completed work or trust a stale checkpoint without inspection. |
| REWIND | Return only the affected work to a known-good state. | Failure point, selected checkpoint and unrelated work boundaries. | Recovered state with unrelated changes preserved. | Never use broad destructive reset when a narrow recovery is possible. |
| SHIELD | Protect a trust boundary, resource, user, secret or critical invariant. | Threat or failure model and protected target. | Enforced prevention, detection and safe failure behaviour. | A warning alone is not a shield when mechanical enforcement is possible. |
| BOUND | Set an explicit limit on scope, authority, resources, time, data or retries. | The unbounded activity and the reason for a limit. | A measurable boundary and behaviour when it is reached. | Do not use an arbitrary bound that makes the required result impossible. |


EVOLUTION OPERATORS

| Operator | Exact use | Required input | Required result | Failure rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CYCLE | Repeat a defined build-check-repair loop. | Repeatable action, evidence check and stop condition. | Iterations that stop on success, a stated limit or a genuine blocker. | Never create an endless loop or repeat unchanged failed work. |
| EVOLVE | Make a deliberate versioned change to behaviour or architecture. | Current contract, desired future contract and migration needs. | Compatible transition, migration path and updated evidence. | Do not silently break supported users or stored data. |
| ENHANCE | Improve an existing capability without unnecessarily changing its public contract. | Existing behaviour, measured weakness and improvement goal. | Better verified behaviour with compatibility preserved. | Do not disguise a breaking change as an enhancement. |
| EXTEND | Add a new capability or supported surface. | Existing boundaries, new requirement and integration points. | New complete behaviour with documentation, checks and failure handling. | Do not leave existing journeys broken or the new surface partly fake. |
| REFINE | Increase clarity, precision, usability, performance or maintainability using feedback. | Working result and concrete evidence or critique. | A measurable improvement with no regression in required behaviour. | Stop refining when the completion bar is met and further work is outside scope. |


CANONICAL COMPUTER-SCIENCE BOUNDARY

The Operator Grammar is a private method, not a product vocabulary.

Before any result is written into the created software, translate it into ordinary, recognised computer-science or product language. Examples include “input validation,” “transactional outbox,” “role-based access control,” “responsive layout,” “health check,” “retry with exponential backoff,” or “database migration.”

The created product must not contain:

- Operator names;
- Spell names;
- Cloth names;
- ORIGIN branding;
- references to a Grimoire, spells, cloths, magical construction or this method; or
- encoded, abbreviated or lightly disguised versions of those terms.

The final product leak check is part of FINALIZE. A detected term is a failed check and must be removed before completion.
