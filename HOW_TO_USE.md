# How to Use the Stellaris Grimoire

The Stellaris Grimoire is a **passive reference system**, not an autonomous agent. The AI interprets and acts on the material; the human may set objectives, direct exploration, challenge results, and curate additions.

## The three files

- **SPELLS.txt:** fictional motifs mapped to computational concepts, implementation analogues, and example uses.
- **CLOTHS.txt:** combined and layered patterns suggesting relationships among capabilities.
- **OPERATORS.txt:** rules for examining, composing, constructing, evolving, and verifying results, including historical research extensions.

Read all three files as references. You do **not** need to use every entry or follow a fixed pipeline.

## Quick start

Attach the three files to an AI assistant capable of reading them. Try:

> Study these three Stellaris Grimoire files as a reference system. I want you to create **[your objective]**. Choose whichever concepts, combinations, and operators are relevant. Translate them into real technical mechanisms, and implement the result where your tools permit. You may explore relationships I haven't specified. Explain what you selected and why, what you actually produced, and what remains unverified. Follow the applicable rules in OPERATORS.txt.

## Three approaches

### 1. Goal-directed construction

Give the AI a concrete task: for example, a persistent simulation, scheduling system, dashboard, or research tool. The AI chooses relevant references, designs a solution, and verifies actual work.

### 2. Open-ended exploration

> Study the three files. Choose an interesting objective of your own. Select and combine whatever references seem useful. Explain your reasoning and produce something concrete. Distinguish implemented results from proposals.

Different models may take different directions; there is no prescribed output.

### 3. Cross-domain translation

Start with a fictional mechanism, real-world process, or unfamiliar discipline. Ask the AI to identify underlying mechanics and translate them into computational structures. The analogy inspires investigation; it does not prove the resulting design works.

## Operators: actions, not incantations

The canonical operator grammar requires five parts for every use:

1. **TARGET** — what is being examined or changed?
2. **INPUT** — which facts, artifacts, or prior results are used?
3. **ACTION** — which defined operation is applied?
4. **RESULT** — what concrete outcome exists?
5. **EVIDENCE** — what supports the outcome?

For example, **CHAIN** connects compatible steps, **LAYER** separates responsibilities, **CHECKPOINT** preserves recoverable progress, and **FINALIZE** requires evidence that applicable completion conditions are met. Merely naming an operator is not completing it.

Read OPERATORS.txt for its full laws and the provenance and limitations of appended research operators. Internal Grimoire names must be translated into ordinary technical language in software products created using the method, as required by the original grammar.

## Illustrative example: persistent simulation

An AI might relate **Preserva** (state preservation) from Spells and **Selene** (temporal scheduling) from Cloths to **CHAIN** and **CHECKPOINT**. It could then design a deterministic event scheduler, persistent storage, save/load controls, and recovery tests. These are *illustrative connections*, not an automatically working implementation: the AI must build and verify the system.

## Revisit and reinterpret

The first selection need not be the last. As you learn more, try:

> Re-examine the Grimoire in light of what we've discovered. Are there relationships or operators that would change your current approach? Explain why before making changes.

Preserve requirements, security boundaries, and existing behaviour unless changes are authorised.

## Historical approaches

The creator and collaborating AI systems have explored structured multi-stage construction, natural-language goal-directed collaboration, open-ended AI-selected projects, fiction-to-computation translation, mathematical investigations, multi-model architectural fusion, and revisiting existing designs after introducing the references. These are examples, not guarantees or mandatory workflows.

## Share your discoveries

We welcome independent applications. Share your objective, selected references, prompts or approach, actual artifacts or observations, evidence where available, and unexpected connections or failures. Distinguish verified findings from interpretations and hypotheses; preserve provenance for proposed new operators.

**Open the book. Choose your direction. Show us what you discover.**
